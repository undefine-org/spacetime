Verified with `tests/repro/gh-6-hold-end-state/`: a one-shot timeline, advanced past its duration with virtual time, keeps both `opacity: 1` and `translateY(-100px)`.

**The mental model.** In Spacetime, animation is driven by *drivers*, and a driver is just a function that produces a progress number between 0 and 1. Scroll position, visibility, elapsed time — all do the same job, which is why they share one head (`@on &.scroll(…)`, `@on &.visible(…)`, `@on &.time(…)`).

Once you see it that way, "holding the end state" stops being a feature to add. It's the answer to a question the system already has to answer: *what does this element look like at p = 1?* The keyframe applier now answers that unconditionally instead of unwinding when the driver completes.

**One caveat I could not test here.** If the driver re-fires from p = 0 — the element leaves the viewport and re-enters — you may see the revert again. That is a different question (re-entry policy, not fill mode), and proving it either way needs a real browser, which this environment doesn't have. It's tracked as BUG-315. If your original case was a curtain that flickers on re-scroll, reopen with that detail and it gets treated as its own issue rather than folded into this one.
