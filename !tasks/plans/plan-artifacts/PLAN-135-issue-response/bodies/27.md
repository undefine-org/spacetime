**Fixed — closing.**

The false `E0923` ambiguity on canonical `@data inline` is gone.

**The structural point, which is the interesting part.** Literal-aware parsing had already *decided*: it selected the `data-inline` form and recorded that decision. The ambiguity lint then **ignored** that result and re-scored from captures alone — and from captures alone, `inline` and `derive` look identical.

So the fix isn't better scoring. It's the lint reading the decision that was already made.

**The general rule:** two passes deriving the same fact independently will eventually disagree, and the one that runs later gets to be wrong in public. If a decision has been made, downstream consumers should *read* it, not recompute it.

That exact pattern shows up three more times in this backlog — #28 (the LSP maintaining a second registry), #30 (definitions re-derived instead of recorded), #32 (the editor approximating the compiler's diagnostics). Same shape every time: a second source of truth that nothing forces to agree with the first.
