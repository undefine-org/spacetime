**Fixed — closing, and it became a feature.**

Raw CSS `@font-face` is now **supported syntax**, handled through the same machinery `@font(...)` uses. Not diagnosed — supported.

**Why the plan changed.** The original intent was to warn and point you at `@font(...)`. Your issue argued the other way, and that argument won: **support what people reach for first.**

`@font-face` is what every CSS author on earth types. A language that recognizes it, understands it, and then refuses it on a naming technicality is spending the author's goodwill to defend an internal preference. That's a bad trade, and it's worse than it looks — it teaches the author that their existing knowledge doesn't transfer, which is the most expensive lesson a new language can teach.

**The design rule this produced**, now written down in `docs/language/css-interop.md`: when a construct exists in the host language (CSS, HTML) and Spacetime has its own spelling for it, **the host spelling must either work or explain itself. Vanishing is off the table.**

That rule is now the thing being enforced, rather than this one instance being patched. If you find another CSS construct that silently disappears, that's a violation of a documented rule and worth filing on those terms.
