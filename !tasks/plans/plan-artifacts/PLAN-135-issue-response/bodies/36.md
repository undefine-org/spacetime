**Fixed — closing, and it was worse than you filed.**

Your report was that optional trailing literal clauses were still required. The repro found that **no** `@wait_until` form worked at all — full, bare, trivial, parenthesized. And `@wait_until false timeout: 500ms` didn't time out; it fell straight through to the next assertion.

Which means anything in the test suite depending on `@wait_until` was asserting **nothing**. The shipped `stdlib/testing/meta/wait-timing.test.st` was failing 9 of 15. It now passes 15/15.

**Your grammar diagnosis was exactly right**, and it's a distinction worth naming precisely: **"has a default" and "may be absent" are different axes.**

A capture's default says what value to use when the clause is *present but unspecified*. It says nothing about whether the literal word `timeout:` may be omitted entirely. Those are independent properties and the grammar only expressed one of them.

**Why splitting into sibling forms doesn't rescue it** (you anticipated this): greedy `expr`/`selector` captures let the shorter form swallow the longer form's keyword. So you don't get two clean alternatives, you get one form eating the other's syntax — the same "consume everything you can, never check what's left" behaviour behind #37 and #35.

The fix gives `%form` real **inline optional groups** — `( timeout : $t:time = 5000 )?` — so optionality is expressed in the grammar itself, as data, rather than duplicated into sibling forms that then compete.
