* Why

Spacetime's TYPES are already data (`%scalar_type` rows, `%capture_type`
productions). Its RELATIONS are not: "a length is acceptable where a
length-percentage is wanted" is knowledge nothing currently writes down, so each
consumer re-derives it or ignores it.

This is the foundation the other maximalist FUPs (188-191) build on. Do it first
or they have nothing to query.

* The model

`≤` — "anywhere a B is wanted, an A will do". Reflexive, transitive, NOT
symmetric. Transitivity is the point: store DIRECT edges as data, derive the
rest by closure, so 14 rows describe every acceptance.

Paired with `~` (consistency, Siek & Taha): reflexive, symmetric, and
DELIBERATELY NOT transitive, so `Unknown ~ length` and `Unknown ~ color` never
license `length ~ color`. Full derivation in
`research/relational-semantics.typ` (already written, on the reMarkable).

Decision procedure, which is the whole checker:

    O ≤ E  → accept, and CLAIM E downstream
    O ~ E  → accept, claim NOTHING
    else   → diagnostic

* THE DISCIPLINE — read before adding any edge

An edge in `≤` is a claim about the WHOLE LANGUAGE, FOREVER. The cautionary tale
is measured and in this repo's history: `number ≤ time` is locally reasonable
(`duration:number = 600` means 600ms) and globally catastrophic — `stagger: 0.05`
is SECONDS, and a blanket numeric migration once made staggers 1000× too slow
across twenty files with a green build.

So: an edge must hold UNCONDITIONALLY, in every directive, in every property.
Anything conditional is a TERNARY relation keyed by the declaration site
(`accepts_unitless(directive, property, policy)`), or it is not a relation.

Add edges ONE AT A TIME, each with a corpus query proving no existing value
changes meaning.

* CORRECTION 2026-08-11 — the carrier moves to %capture_type

Filed with `%widens_to` rows on `%scalar_type`. That is the wrong carrier. See
`research/syntax-inference.typ` Part IV (on the reMarkable) for the full
argument; the short form:

`%scalar_type` is ATTRIBUTIVE — 14 rows hanging meaning and widgets onto
categories that already exist. `%capture_type` is CONSTITUTIVE — a value IS its
production. The thing that has an EXTENSION, and can therefore be compared, is
the production.

** What changes

  as filed:     %widens_to on %scalar_type,  14 rows, relation over scalars
  as corrected: %widens_to on %capture_type,          relation over productions

Scalars still get the relation, because a scalar type NAMES a production. But so
do references, holes, wide keywords and directive refs — which under the filed
design sat outside the relation entirely, because they are not scalars.

That is the whole argument: `--ink` has no place in a scalar relation, and
`--ink` is exactly the value this arc has spent weeks failing to type.

** What it buys in verification

A declared edge over productions can be CHECKED against language inclusion
(grammar A ≤ B iff everything A matches, B matches — decidable for grammars this
small). So:

  - declared edges stay honest about INTENT (no accidental widenings from two
    grammars that happen to overlap)
  - derivation makes a WRONG claim impossible (assert every declared edge is a
    real inclusion)

Strictly better than either alone. Add this as a gate:
`tests/consistency_relation_test.rs::every_declared_edge_is_a_real_inclusion`.

Scalar rows cannot support this check at all — they have no extension to
compare. That asymmetry is the tell.

** What does NOT change

The `~` relation, its deliberate non-transitivity, the `O ≤ E` / `O ~ E`
decision procedure, THE DISCIPLINE section above, and every verification seed.
Those were right. Only the carrier moves, and it moves DOWN onto the thing that
was already primary.
