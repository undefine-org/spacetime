* Why

Several `%form`s can match the same directive. Today the winner is chosen by a
SCORING FUNCTION — a hand-tuned heuristic that assigns points and takes the
maximum. A heuristic answers "which felt best"; it cannot answer "which is
strictly more specific", and it cannot explain a tie.

With `≤` (FUP-189) specificity stops being a feeling and becomes an ORDER: form
A is more specific than form B when every parameter of A is `≤` the
corresponding parameter of B. The winner is the least element — a JOIN, not a
maximum score.

* What this fixes that scoring cannot

- A TIE becomes detectable and reportable. Two forms neither of which is `≤` the
  other are genuinely ambiguous, and today one silently wins by point ordering.
- The choice becomes EXPLAINABLE: "form A won because `length ≤
  length_percentage`" is a path through the relation, printable in a diagnostic.
- Adding a scalar can no longer perturb unrelated resolutions, which a shared
  point scale can.

should be substantially SMALLER or gone. If it survives intact alongside
`subtype()`, that is a parallel implementation and the work is not finished —
grep `grep -rn "form_scoring\|fn score" --include=*.rs src/` and account for
every remaining caller.
