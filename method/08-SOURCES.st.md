**Status vocabulary:**

- `replicated` — experimental finding, independently reproduced or meta-analysed
- `experiment` — primary experimental result; replication not established here
- `framework` — theoretical/interpretive model — organises thinking, does not predict
- `practitioner` — working method from a named originator; testimonial evidence
- `folklore` — widely repeated, attribution or evidence unverified
- `doctrine` — legal/professional standard, not a scientific claim


## Reader completion

**S01 — Iser, "The Reading Process: A Phenomenological Approach" (1972)**, *New
Literary History* 3(2), 279–299 · <http://www.jstor.org/stable/468316> ·
books: *The Implied Reader* (1974), *The Act of Reading* (1978).
`framework` — gaps/blanks (*Leerstellen*) activate the reader; a text that
specifies everything produces boredom. **The strongest articulation of the
completion mechanic — and it is literary theory, not marketing evidence.**

**S02 — Ingarden, *The Literary Work of Art*** (1931/1960).
`framework` — "places of indeterminacy" and reader concretization; the source
Iser builds on.

**S06 — Zwaan & Radvansky (1998)**, *Psych. Bulletin* 123(2) ·
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.123.2.162>.
`framework` — comprehension constructs integrated situation models. Grounds
[R2](/05-COPY) (scene over adjective). *Caveat: "comprehension IS simulation" is
stronger than the evidence; treat as dominant framework.*

**S23 — Barsalou (1999), Perceptual Symbol Systems** ·
<https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X99002149>.
`framework` — conceptual processing recruits perceptual reenactment. Why
imageable language works.

**S24 — Slamecka & Graf (1978), generation effect** ·
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.4.6.592>.
`replicated` — self-generated material retained better than read material.
*Measures memory, not conviction — transfer to copy is analogy.*

**S08 — Rogers, Kuiper & Kirker (1977), self-reference effect** ·
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.35.9.677>.
`replicated` — self-related encoding improves recall.

**S25 — Symons & Johnson (1997), meta-analysis, 129 studies** ·
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.121.3.371>.
`replicated` — self-reference robust, **smaller** against elaborative/
organisational controls. Cited to keep S08 honest.

**S26 — "Concrete structure, open instance"** — our formula
([05-COPY](/05-COPY)).
`framework` — **a synthesis of S01/S06/S08/S24, not a validated named law.**
Stated as a working rule, and testable. Flagged because it is the method's most
load-bearing invention.

**S32 — Green & Brock (2000), transportation** ·
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701>.
`experiment` — transported readers counter-argue less. Bounded in
[05-COPY](/05-COPY): reduced counter-arguing ≠ truthful persuasion.

**S29 — Loewenstein (1994), information-gap theory of curiosity** ·
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75>.
`framework` — curiosity needs a *perceived, bounded* gap. The respectable
version of "curiosity gap"; grounds [R5](/05-COPY).

**S30 — Zeigarnik (1927)**; modern review ·
<https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w>.
`folklore` **for the memory claim** — the meta-analysis reports **no general
unfinished-task memory advantage.** Listed to forbid its use.

**S33 — Eisenberg (1984), strategic ambiguity** ·
<https://doi.org/10.1080/03637758409390197>.
`framework` — ambiguity enabling multiple audiences ("unified diversity").
Theoretical; not landing-page evidence.


## The Barnum boundary

**S27 — Forer (1949), "The fallacy of personal validation"**, *J. Abnormal &
Social Psychology*.
`experiment` — students accepted a generic sketch as personally accurate.
*Small classroom sample (39); a demonstration, not a population estimate.
Original full text not accessed in this session — the 13-item sketch circulating
online should be checked against the article scan before being quoted.*

**S34 — Meehl (1956)** · <https://doi.org/10.1037/h0044164>.
`framework` — coined "Barnum effect".

**S35 — Snyder & Larson (1972)** · <https://doi.org/10.1037/h0032899>.
`experiment` — generic descriptions accepted **more** when framed as personalised.

**S36 — Dickson & Kelly (1985), Barnum moderators review** ·
<https://doi.org/10.2466/pr0.1985.57.2.367>.
`framework` — favourability, relevance, source authority, generality interact.
*No single universal effect size.*

**S28 — Puffery doctrine** (advertising law).
`doctrine` — vague subjective superiority is non-actionable because no reasonable
consumer relies on it; specific/measurable claims require substantiation. Used as
an external proxy for "is this sentence empty?" *Varies by jurisdiction. Legal
non-actionability does not make a line true or useful.*

**S31 — Specificity & credibility**: Ganz & Grimes (2018)
<https://doi.org/10.2501/JAR-2018-001>; Thomas, Simon & Kadiyali (2010)
<https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1090.0512>.
`experiment` — claim specificity / numerical precision can raise perceived
credibility and shift judgement. **Not a licence for fake precision** —
grounds [R6](/05-COPY).


## Why elicitation is needed at all

**S12 — Nisbett & Wilson (1977), "Telling More Than We Can Know"** ·
<https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.3.231>.
`replicated` — people may have little direct access to their own higher-order
cognitive processes; causal self-reports often reflect plausible a priori
theories. *The paper's actual claim is narrower than the pop version "people
never know why" — use it to downgrade unverified causal explanations, not to
dismiss all self-report.* Grounds [P2](/00-PRINCIPLES).

**S13 — Johansson et al., choice blindness** (2005 *Science*; 2006 follow-up) ·
<https://www.lucs.lu.se/fileadmin/user_upload/lucs/2011/01/Johansson-et-al.-2006-How-Something-Can-Be-Said-About-Telling-More-Than-We-Can-Know.pdf>.
`experiment` — covertly swapped outcomes were detected in **no more than 26%** of
manipulation trials; participants then supplied introspective reasons for choices
they had not made, and the 2006 analysis found very few linguistic differences
between manipulated and genuine reports. *A controlled face-choice task — it
bounds introspective reliability; it does not prove every business answer false.*


## Elicitation technique

**S05 — Lawley & Tompkins, Clean Language / Symbolic Modelling** ·
<https://cleanlanguage.com/clean-language-revisited/> · *Metaphors in Mind*
(2000); originator **David Grove**.
`practitioner` — the 9+3 questions; facilitator uses **only** the client's words
to avoid contaminating with their own metaphors/assumptions/presuppositions.
Grove's observation: ordinary questions gave a client's metaphor "a short
half-life." **The method's anti-leading backbone** ([P3](/00-PRINCIPLES)).
*Caveat, from the originators: clean ≠ no influence. Choosing which phrase to
ask about still directs attention.*

**S04 — Erickson case literature**; Zeig, *The Virtues of Our Faults*.
`practitioner` — **utilization**: use whatever the client brings as the vehicle.
Documented core of Erickson's practice; adopted in [P10](/00-PRINCIPLES).

**S03 — Bandler & Grinder, *Patterns of the Hypnotic Techniques of Milton H.
Erickson***; critical review: Passmore & Rowson (2019).
`folklore` **as science** — the "Milton Model" taxonomy is B&G's *reconstruction*
of Erickson, not his validated theory; NLP's distinctive claims have weak-to-
negative empirical support. **Compliance patterns are excluded from the interview
by [P10](/00-PRINCIPLES)** — they manufacture agreement, which is the failure
signal in Pass 1.

**S07 — de Shazer / de Jong & Berg, solution-focused brief therapy.**
`practitioner` — miracle question ("if a miracle happened overnight and the
problem were solved, how would you know?"), scaling, exception-finding. Descends
from Erickson. Used in §1.2/§1.8. *Elicits desired-state description — not
forecasts, not feasibility evidence.*


## Interview methodology

**S09 — Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*** · <https://www.momtestbook.com/>.
`practitioner` — talk about their life not your idea; past specifics not future
opinions; listen more. *The bad→good rewrites in [01](/01-INTERVIEW) are our
adaptations — the official page does not publish the book's examples verbatim.*

**S10 — Moesta & Spiek, JTBD switch interview** ·
<https://jobstobedone.org/switch-interview/> · forces diagram transcript
<https://jobstobedone.org/radio/unpacking-the-progress-making-forces-diagram/>.
`practitioner` — timeline reconstruction (first thought → passive → active →
decision → first use); four forces (push/pull/anxiety/habit) inferred from
events, never asked about directly. *Interview counts, recency windows, and the
"80% noise / 20% energy" heuristic are Moesta's practitioner guidance, not
validated laws.*

**S20 — Reynolds & Gutman (1988), laddering / means-end chain**, *J. Advertising
Research* · <https://doi.org/10.1080/00218499.1988.12467766>.
`practitioner` — attribute → consequence → value via repeated "why is that
important to you?"; ladders aggregate into hierarchical value maps.
*Failure mode: fatigue and confabulation under repeated probing — hence the
citation gate in [D4](/03-POSITIONING).*

**S16 — Wiebe / Copyhackers, message mining** ·
<https://copyhackers.com/join-the-conversation-customer/>; Havice-adjacent VoC
guide.
`practitioner` — copy is assembled from customer language. Canonical prompt:
*"What was going on in your life that brought you to try/join/buy [product]
today?"* *Caveat: self-selected respondents, post-purchase gratitude,
survivorship. "Sticky" ≠ representative.*

**S11 — Cooperrider & Srivastva, Appreciative Inquiry.**
`practitioner` — affirmative framing surfaces the "positive core"; 4-D cycle.
Used once, in §1.7. *Wording located in practitioner material, not verified
against the original publication. Positivity bias — pair with premortem.*

**S18 — Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem", HBR (2007)** ·
<https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem> ·
<https://www.gary-klein.com/premortem>.
`practitioner` — prospective hindsight makes dissent psychologically safe.
Grounds §2.2. *A generative-risk device; imagined causes are not probabilities.*

**S37 — Indi Young, listening sessions / mental models** ·
<https://indiyoung.com/>.
`practitioner` — problem space vs solution space; person-and-purpose before
product. Warns against demographic archetypes and the mythical average user.


## Positioning & copy strategy

**S14 — Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*** (1966).
`practitioner` — copy cannot create desire, only channel existing mass desire.
Five **awareness** stages × five **sophistication** stages — **the highest-
leverage diagnostic in this method** ([D2](/03-POSITIONING)).
*Important: the detailed stage→headline mappings in wide circulation are
operational reconstructions, not verbatim from an accessible primary scan. Treat
the two axes as Schwartz-derived, the mapping tables as our operationalisation.*

**S15 — Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* / quickstart guide** ·
<https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning>; Ries &
Trout, *Positioning*; Neumeier, *Zag* (onlyness); Moore, positioning template.
`practitioner` — positioning derives from **competitive alternatives first**
(including spreadsheets, manual work, doing nothing), then unique attributes →
value → who cares → category. Dunford's explicit warning: template-first
positioning ("Mad Lib") assumes one right answer per blank and is dangerous;
category creation imposes buyer education cost.

**S19 — Ramadan/Peterson/Lochhead, *Play Bigger*** ·
<https://www.playbigger.com/categorydesign>.
`practitioner`, **promotional** — category design, POV, from/to framing,
"lightning strike". Useful for from/to contrast questions. *Claims about category
kings and cognitive conditioning are not established causal science; Dunford's
education-cost warning is the counterweight.*

**S17 — Cialdini, *Influence*** ·
<https://www.influenceatwork.com/principles-of-persuasion-are-not-just-for-business/>.
`framework` — reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, liking,
authority, scarcity (+ unity, later). Used **only** as an elicitation checklist
([P9](/00-PRINCIPLES)) — inventory what the business actually possesses.
*Related real evidence for yes-ladders: Freedman & Fraser (1966)
foot-in-the-door — distinct from any hypnotic claim.*

**S21 — Mark & Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw*** (12 archetypes).
`folklore` **as segmentation** — Jungian archetype theory has contested empirical
status. Usable as a late coherence check; never as an input, never as a quiz
([D6](/03-POSITIONING)).

**S22 — Miller, StoryBrand SB7**; critique:
<https://drmichellemazur.com/2024/07/4-reasons-storybrand-framework-didnt-work-for-your-business/>.
`practitioner` — customer-as-hero, brand-as-guide, clear plan. Useful information
architecture; **produces interchangeable pages when used as a generator.** Check,
not input.

**S38 — Strategyzer, Value Proposition Canvas** ·
<https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-proposition-canvas>.
`framework` — jobs/pains/gains ↔ pain relievers/gain creators. A discovery
canvas; not a positioning statement.

**S39 — Direct-response craft**: Ogilvy
(<https://www.ogilvy.com/sites/g/files/dhpsjz106/files/pdfdocuments/Quotations_of_David_Ogilvy_v2.pdf>)
— "boil down your strategy to one simple promise", "what you show is more
important than what you say"; Sugarman (slippery slide); Caples (tested
headlines); Hopkins (advertising as measured salesmanship).
`practitioner`. **Halbert's "starving crowd" and the Schwab headline anecdote
are `folklore`** — heuristics, not controlled results. Extract the diagnostic
question, never the statistic.

**S40 — Vitale, *Hypnotic Writing*; Hogan; Warren; "Sullivan Nod"; Collier's
"enter the conversation already occurring in the reader's mind."**
`folklore` — practitioner claims resting on testimonial evidence. Where these
work, they reduce to ordinary mechanisms already sourced above (attention,
relevance, imageability, consistency, message-market fit). *Collier's famous
line is commonly attributed; primary textual verification not obtained.*
**Listed to be explicit that the method does not rest on them.**


## A note on this file

Several entries name claims the method **refuses** to use (S03 compliance
patterns, S30 Zeigarnik, S21 archetypes-as-segmentation, S40 hypnotic writing).
They are here deliberately: a sources list that only contains load-bearing
citations hides the reasoning that rejected the rest, and the next agent will
re-import the folklore we filtered.

Where a source was not accessed in primary form, that is stated at the entry
rather than smoothed over ([P1](/00-PRINCIPLES)).
