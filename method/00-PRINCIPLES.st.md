## P1 — Evidence before interpretation

Three classes of statement exist, and they are never blended:

Columns: tag · meaning · who owns it.

- **`observed`** — *a thing that happened. Dated, attributable, in principle checkable.* → owned by the world
- **`stated`** — *the human's own words, verbatim, unparaphrased.* → owned by the human
- **`inferred`** — *a synthesis, hypothesis, or pattern-claim.* → owned by the agent

An `inferred` claim may be used, argued for, and shipped — but it may never be
*presented as* `observed`. The v1 failure this method exists to prevent was
exactly this collapse: an agent's inference about SMB pain was typeset inside
quotation marks, which is the visual grammar of testimony.

**Rule.** Quotation marks are reserved for `observed` or `stated`. An `inferred`
pain is written as an assertion in the brand's own voice, never as speech.


## P2 — Introspection is not evidence

The human being interviewed cannot reliably report *why* they do what they do.
This is not a comment on any individual; it is a finding about people
(Nisbett & Wilson 1977 — [S12](/08-SOURCES); Johansson et al. choice blindness
— [S13](/08-SOURCES): participants failed to notice their chosen outcome had
been swapped in most manipulation trials, then fluently justified the choice
they had not made).

∴ **Ask for events, not reasons.** "Why do you think customers want this?"
produces confident, plausible, unreliable output. "Tell me about the last time
you watched this break — what happened next?" produces material.

Reasons the human volunteers are recorded as `stated` — valuable as *language*,
never as *causation*.


## P3 — The interviewer's words are contamination

Grove's finding ([S05](/08-SOURCES)): when a facilitator asked ordinary
questions about a client's metaphor, the metaphor had "a short half-life" — it
evaporated and the client reverted to generic conceptual description. Only
questions built from the client's *exact words* kept it alive.

For our case, the practical translation: **when the human says something raw
and specific, the agent's instinct is to paraphrase it into marketing
language — and that paraphrase destroys the asset.** The raw phrase is the
thing worth having. Marketing language is what everyone else already has.

**Rule.** In Pass 1, the agent introduces no nouns of its own. Questions are
built from the human's words. Verbatims are recorded before any paraphrase, and
the paraphrase is stored *beside* the original, never over it.


## P4 — Two passes, and they never mix

**Pass 1 · Clean** against **Pass 2 · Adversarial**, dimension by dimension:

- **goal** — Pass 1: get the raw material uncontaminated · Pass 2: break the hypotheses
- **agent's vocabulary** — Pass 1: forbidden · Pass 2: required
- **agent's hypotheses** — Pass 1: withheld · Pass 2: stated plainly for attack
- **stance** — Pass 1: curious, slow, follows the human · Pass 2: challenging, premortem, seeks disconfirmation
- **failure if skipped** — Pass 1: copy repeats the agent's clichés back · Pass 2: untested inferences ship as fact

Running them simultaneously is the natural instinct and it destroys both: a
hypothesis offered mid-elicitation becomes the thing the human responds to, and
you have interviewed yourself.


## P5 — Gaps are recorded, never filled

When the record lacks something a page needs, three moves are legitimate:

1. ask the human,
2. mark the claim `inferred` and let it stand as an argued position,
3. **cut the section.**

Inventing the missing evidence is not on the list. A page with four honest
sections beats a page with seven where three are fiction — because the three
are indistinguishable from the four to every reader, including the human who
approved them.

**Corollary for young products.** A product with no users yet cannot show
customer proof. It can show: the mechanism, the founder's own observed
experience, a live demonstration, and an explicit statement of what has not
been proven. Concealing youth is a claim; stating it is evidence of the
disposition the buyer is actually assessing.


## P6 — The reader completes the sentence; the frame constrains the completion

The target experience — *"this is EXACTLY what I need"*, different per reader —
is produced by **constrained co-construction**: the text supplies a structure,
the reader supplies the referent ([S01](/08-SOURCES) Iser; [S06](/08-SOURCES)
situation models; [S08](/08-SOURCES) self-reference).

The constraint is what separates it from horoscope copy. Honest completion
narrows the admissible fillings to situations the product genuinely handles.
Barnum copy permits any flattering filling at all ([S14](/08-SOURCES) Forer).

**Rule.** Every completion line declares its frame and its slot. If an agent
cannot name which part is the frame, the line is not a completion line — it is
vagueness, and it fails the lint in [05-COPY](/05-COPY).


## P7 — Segment by situation, not by industry

Industries are a labelling convenience. What actually predicts whether someone
needs the product is the **situation they are in** — the trigger, the
constraint, the workaround they've already improvised.

Two hotels can be in different situations; a hotel and a clinic can be in the
same one. Naming the industry feels concrete and is often the *less* precise
move, because it imports a hundred assumptions that don't hold and excludes
readers who would have completed the sentence correctly.

∴ Pages are organised by situation. An industry page is legitimate **only when
the situation genuinely differs**, or when it is the reader's own search
vocabulary — and then the page's job is to route them to the situation as fast
as possible. See [04-SITEMAP](/04-SITEMAP).

**Corollary — market preconditions are part of the segment.** If the product
depends on an environmental fact (a channel's penetration, a regulation, a
device), that fact is a **qualifier**, not a footnote. Naming it excludes
readers, which is precisely why it makes the remaining readers trust the page.


## P8 — The page is held to the product's own standard

Whatever the brand claims about itself, its own website must exemplify.

A product about evidence may not fabricate testimony. A product about
simplicity may not ship a confusing page. A product about speed may not load
slowly.

This is not decoration; it is the first and most costly demonstration, because
the visitor experiences it *before* believing anything you say.


## P9 — Persuasion assets are inventory, not technique

Cialdini's principles ([S17](/08-SOURCES)) are used here as an **elicitation
checklist**, never as a licence to manufacture:

- authority → *what expertise do you actually possess, and where does it end?*
- social proof → *which comparable customer got which result — with denominator and date?*
- scarcity → *what is genuinely limited: capacity, timing, access?*
- reciprocity → *what genuinely useful thing can we give before asking?*

Each answers with a real asset or with nothing. Fabricated scarcity and
borrowed authority buy compliance now and distrust later, and they are the
easiest thing in the world for an agent to generate.


## P10 — Ericksonian technique: utilization in, compliance patterns out

The interview borrows Erickson's **utilization** — take whatever the human
brings (their resistance, their offhand metaphor, their objection) and make it
the vehicle ([S04](/08-SOURCES)). This is the documented core of his practice
and it is the engine of Clean Language.

The interview **rejects** the Milton-model compliance patterns — presupposition,
embedded command, yes-set, double bind. Reason: those patterns are engineered
to *produce agreement*. Aimed at the human during elicitation they manufacture
consent to whatever the agent already believes, contaminating the exact answers
we are trying to get clean. They are also, as a body of theory, a secondary
reconstruction of Erickson rather than his validated method, with weak empirical
support ([S03](/08-SOURCES)).

The distinction is not squeamishness. It is that agreement is the *failure
signal* in Pass 1. If the human keeps agreeing, the agent is talking.

Downstream, in copy aimed at a *visitor*, indirection and imagery are legitimate
craft — bounded by [05-COPY](/05-COPY)'s lint and by P8.
